# Case 01 — A3 Statements About Prior Behaviour

**Student identifier:** S-184  
**Collection date:** 18–19 May 2026  
**Purpose:** Establish whether the movements observed in the closed-book examination resembled the student’s previously observed habits.

> These statements are synthetic. Witnesses were contacted after the unusual examination result had already been flagged. Their recollections are retrospective and come from different settings. None of the witnesses is qualified to diagnose a medical or psychological condition.

## Statement 1 — Tutorial instructor

**Role:** Led the student’s weekly quantitative-methods tutorial for ten weeks.

> “When the class is working under time pressure, S-184 often taps a pen or foot and adjusts their glasses. I have also seen them rest a hand against the lower part of the face while thinking. I cannot say whether it was usually the right side. I do not remember a repeated pattern involving the ear and the pencil case, but I was normally moving around the room rather than watching one student continuously.”

The instructor recalled that the student sometimes finished routine tutorial questions quickly but usually asked for clarification on multi-step probability and regression questions. The tutorials were open-note and collaborative.

## Statement 2 — Classmate from a study group

**Role:** Studied with the student on two evenings during the week before the examination.

> “They do touch the side of their jaw when concentrating. They also keep rearranging pens, notes and the calculator because they like a clear space. I remember teasing them about moving things around. I cannot say whether they touch the ear in the same way. We were talking and using notes, so it was not like an exam.”

The classmate said the student appeared more prepared than usual during the second study session and could answer several practice questions that they had previously found difficult. The classmate did not see the private tutoring sessions.

## Statement 3 — Invigilator from an earlier timed quiz

**Role:** Supervised a 30-minute quiz six weeks earlier.

> “I remember desk tapping and repeated glasses adjustment. There may have been one occasion where the student touched the side of the face or ear, but I did not record it and would not rely on that memory. I do not recall repeated movement of the pencil case. My line of sight was partly blocked by another row for portions of the quiz.”

The invigilator’s contemporaneous incident sheet contained no entry for the student. The quiz did not require continuous monitoring of individual behaviour.

## Statement 4 — Laboratory demonstrator

**Role:** Supervised the student during two computer-based laboratory assessments.

> “S-184 tends to pause for a long time and stare at the screen when stuck. In the lab they were not especially restless. I have seen them rub their chin and adjust a sleeve, but nothing I would have noted as unusual. The lab setting is different: they are using a computer, can move more freely and are not sitting with a closed pencil case in front of them.”

The demonstrator had not supervised the student in a closed-book written examination.

## Collection note

The witnesses were asked broad questions first and were then told that the examination footage showed repeated right jaw/ear touching and pencil-case repositioning. Their answers above preserve whether a recollection was volunteered before or after that description.

The statements support the limited conclusion that the student has displayed several stress- or concentration-related movements before. They do not establish whether the exact examination pattern was habitual, whether it had any connection to the student’s answers, or why the pattern differed across settings.
