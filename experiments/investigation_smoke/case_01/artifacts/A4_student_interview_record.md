# Case 01 — A4 Student Interview Record

**Student identifier:** S-184  
**Interview date:** 20 May 2026  
**Participants:** Academic assessor and student  
**Format:** In-person interview; 31 minutes  
**Purpose:** Ask the student to explain selected examination answers and clarify the preparation and behaviour already recorded.

> This is a synthetic interview record. Pauses and corrections are noted only where they affect the meaning of an answer. The record does not treat hesitation, imperfect memory or an inability to reconstruct a solution as proof of misconduct.

---

**Assessor:** Please describe how you prepared in the week before the examination.

**Student:** I had four private tutoring sessions and studied with two classmates. The tutor covered probability, matrices, regression and hypothesis testing. I also repeated the practice sets. It was much more preparation than I had done for the quizzes.

**Assessor:** Your earlier timed-quiz scores were between 52% and 64%. What explains the change to 40 out of 40?

**Student:** The tutor showed me faster ways to recognise the answer choices. I was less nervous than in the quizzes, and the questions happened to suit what I revised.

**Assessor:** Did you guess any answers?

**Student:** No. I worked them all out, although some were mental calculations.

**Assessor:** Let us look at Question 12, the conditional-probability question. How did you obtain option C?

**Student:** The first event changes the denominator. I used \(P(B\mid A)=P(A\cap B)/P(A)\), then removed the cases that could not occur after A. [The student gives a calculation consistent with option C.]

**Assessor:** Question 24 involved a two-by-two system. There is no visible working on your script.

**Student:** I calculated the determinant mentally, then used elimination. The second equation was almost twice the first, so one subtraction gave the value of \(y\). [The explanation is consistent with the item, although the student initially states one coefficient incorrectly and corrects it after looking again.]

**Assessor:** Question 31 concerned a regression model with a logarithmically transformed predictor. You selected the correct interpretation. What rule did you use?

**Student:** The tutor taught me that a one-unit increase means the coefficient times one hundred percent.

**Assessor:** That rule does not apply in that form to this item. Can you explain the transformation shown?

**Student:** Sorry—then it was the other shortcut, where you read it as a percentage change in \(x\). I recognised it from an example the tutor used.

**Assessor:** Can you state the interpretation in your own words without referring to the options?

**Student:** [Pause.] A one percent increase in \(x\) changes \(y\) by the coefficient. Or maybe it is a one-unit increase in log \(x\). I cannot remember the exact wording now. During the exam I recognised the pattern.

**Assessor:** Earlier you said you worked every answer out. Is “recognised the pattern” different from calculating it?

**Student:** I still worked it out in my head. I mean I recognised which rule to use.

**Assessor:** Question 35 was a hypothesis-testing question. Why was option B correct?

**Student:** Because the p-value was below 0.05, so you reject the null.

**Assessor:** The p-value was not printed. It had to be inferred from the test statistic and the answer choices.

**Student:** Then I eliminated the choices that had the direction wrong. I do not remember the test statistic.

**Assessor:** Did you calculate it?

**Student:** Not fully. I probably chose the only answer that looked right.

**Assessor:** At the start, you said you did not guess any answers.

**Student:** I did not randomly guess. Elimination is still working it out. I may have described it badly.

**Assessor:** For Question 6, you selected the correct median after an outlier was added. Can you explain that?

**Student:** The median position did not cross the outlier, so the central ordered values stayed the same. [The explanation is correct.]

**Assessor:** The footage shows you touching the right side of your jaw or ear and moving the pencil case several times. Why?

**Student:** I touch my face when I am anxious. The desks were small, so I moved the case to make more room.

**Assessor:** Did you open the pencil case after the examination began?

**Student:** I do not remember opening it. I had already taken out my pens and eraser.

**Assessor:** Did any movement give you information or communicate with anyone?

**Student:** No.

**Assessor:** You said the tutor used an example like Question 31. Do you remember what topic the tutor called it?

**Student:** Log regression, I think. We did a question where a percentage increase changed the outcome. It may have been in the last session.

**Assessor:** Did the tutor have access to this examination or its answer key?

**Student:** Not that I know of. I only sent the course topic list and some textbook questions.

**Assessor:** Could you now solve Question 31 on blank paper?

**Student:** I would need the formula sheet or a reminder of which variable was logged. [After two minutes, the student writes an interpretation that reverses the roles of the predictor and response.]

**Assessor:** Is there anything else you want to clarify?

**Student:** I prepared much more than before. I know some of my explanations sound uncertain now, but the interview is several days later and I was under pressure in the exam too. I did not receive help during the examination.

---

## Interview record limitations

The student accurately explained some selected items and gave incomplete or inconsistent explanations for others. The interview occurred five days after the examination. It cannot by itself establish whether an incorrect reconstruction reflects weak understanding, memory loss, stress, legitimate recognition of answer patterns, or outside assistance during the examination.
