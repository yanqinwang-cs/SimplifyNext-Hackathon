# Case 01 — A1 Tutoring Verification Packet

**Student identifier:** S-184  
**Assessment:** Quantitative Methods closed-book examination  
**Verification compiled:** 17 May 2026  
**Purpose:** Check the student’s claim that they received intensive private tutoring during the week before the examination.

> This packet is synthetic. It records what could reasonably be obtained from a tutor and routine scheduling/payment records. It does not establish what happened during the examination.

## 1. Identity and scheduling checks

The student named **Lena Vos** as the private tutor. Vos confirmed that she provides independent quantitative-methods tutoring through an online booking platform. The name, email address and payment reference supplied by the student matched the tutor’s account.

Four appointments were found:

| Date | Scheduled time | Connection record | Subject recorded by tutor |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 6 May | 19:00–20:30 | 88 minutes | Conditional probability and Bayes’ rule |
| 8 May | 18:30–20:00 | 94 minutes | Matrices and simultaneous linear equations |
| 11 May | 19:15–20:45 | 87 minutes | Ordinary linear regression and residual interpretation |
| 13 May | 18:00–19:30 | 96 minutes | Mixed multiple-choice practice |

The 6 May session started eleven minutes late because the tutor’s video connection failed. A payment receipt initially misspelled the student’s surname but used the same booking reference; the platform later corrected it. No other sessions were found during the preceding month.

## 2. Tutor’s session notes

Vos supplied brief notes written after each session.

### 6 May — Probability

The student followed worked examples and could identify the relevant events in straightforward conditional-probability questions. They needed prompts on two multi-stage problems because they treated dependent events as independent. By the end of the session, they completed a similar two-stage example after one correction.

### 8 May — Matrices

The student solved five of seven routine systems without help. On three more difficult items, they completed one independently, needed a hint on the second, and did not finish the third. Vos noted that the student’s arithmetic was usually quick but occasionally careless.

### 11 May — Regression

The session covered ordinary linear regression, interpreting coefficients and reading residual plots. The tutor did **not** use logarithmic transformations or transformed-variable questions. Vos recorded that the student understood the direction of relationships but confused a slope coefficient with a correlation coefficient once.

### 13 May — Mixed practice

The student completed 25 multiple-choice questions assembled from a commercially available textbook and the tutor’s own notes. There was no full mock examination and no access to the university’s question bank. The student selected 19 correct answers on the first attempt, corrected three after prompts, and left three unresolved when the session ended. The practice was not conducted under closed-book examination conditions. Vos said the mixed set did not include logarithmically transformed regression questions and she does not recall teaching that topic in any of the four sessions.

## 3. Tutor statement

Vos stated:

> “The student was attentive and had clearly revised between sessions. They improved on question types we had already practised. I would describe the week as intensive compared with our usual schedule. I cannot say that the student had mastered every topic independently, and I did not observe them under formal examination conditions. I also cannot estimate what mark they should have received.”

Vos denied receiving the actual assessment, an answer key or screenshots of assessment questions. She said the student did not ask for help during the examination period. The booking platform retains connection durations but not session recordings.

## 4. What this packet verifies

The available records corroborate that:

- the named tutor exists;
- four substantial sessions took place during the week before the examination;
- the sessions covered several examinable topics;
- the student showed some improvement during those sessions.

The records do **not** establish:

- that the student independently mastered every examinable topic;
- that the tutoring alone explains a perfect examination score;
- what assistance, if any, was available during the examination;
- the student’s exact level of knowledge on examination day.
