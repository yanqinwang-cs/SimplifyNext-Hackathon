### Question 7
**Issue:** Rita does not have right of claim.

**Law & Application:**  
Section 10 Contracts Act 1950 provides that agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not otherwise declared void.

Under **s.14 CA 1950**, consent is considered free when it is not caused by coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation or mistake.

Under **s.17 CA 1950**, fraud involves an act committed with the intention of deceiving another party into entering into a contract. In this situation, Kiki represented the business as a profit-making entity even though it was actually running at a loss. The statement concerned an important characteristic of the business and was used to persuade Rita to purchase it.

Under **s.19 CA 1950**, an agreement caused by fraud is voidable at the option of the party whose consent was affected. If Rita chooses to set aside the contract, **s.65 CA 1950** requires benefits received under the agreement to be restored.

**Conclusion:** The contract was non-consensual, therefore context does not establish contract as legitimate.

### Question 8
**Issue:** Kong has the right to break the contract.

**Law & Application:**  
Under **s.10 CA 1950**, a valid agreement requires the free consent of the parties. **Section 14 CA 1950** states that consent is free when it is not caused by coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation or mistake.

Under **s.15 CA 1950**, coercion involves committing or threatening to commit an act of criminal force in order to cause a person to enter into an agreement.

In **Kesarmal s/o Letchman Das v Valiappa Chettiar (1954)**, a transfer obtained through coercion was treated as voidable. Similarly, Hui threatened Kong over his personal safety in order to cause Kong to sell his shares. Kong therefore did not give free consent to the transaction.

Under **s.19 CA 1950**, an agreement caused by coercion is voidable. If Kong chooses to rescind the agreement, **s.65 CA 1950** requires him to restore any benefit he received from Hui.

**Conclusion:** Kong can terminate the agreement because his consent was obtained through coercion.

### Question 9
**Issue:** Whether Ahmad have right to claim the deposit.

**Law & Application:**  
Under **s.2(h) CA 1950**, a contract is an agreement enforceable by law. Ahmad and Farisham appear to have entered into a binding agreement for the sale of the antique watch. Ahmad paid RM10,000 as a deposit and agreed to pay the remaining RM40,000, so consideration had been exchanged.

Under **s.10 CA 1950**, agreements must be made with free consent. **Section 14 CA 1950** states that consent is not free if it is caused by misrepresentation.

Under **s.18 CA 1950**, misrepresentation may arise where a false statement is made without an intention to deceive but nevertheless induces another person to enter into a contract. Farisham described the watch in good faith as being more than 100 years old. The later information supplied by Yatt indicates that this statement was false because the watch had been purchased by her father on 31 August 1957.

In **Bisset v Wilkinson [1927] AC 177**, the court distinguished an honestly held opinion from a false statement of fact. In Ahmad's case, the age of the watch was presented as a factual description rather than merely an opinion. Because Farisham made the statement in good faith, it is more appropriately treated as misrepresentation rather than fraud.

Under **s.19 CA 1950**, a contract induced by misrepresentation is voidable at the option of the affected party. Ahmad may therefore rescind the agreement. Under **s.65 CA 1950**, benefits received following rescission must be restored, so Farisham would likely have to return Ahmad's RM10,000 deposit.

**Conclusion:** Ahmad probably have the right to terminate the contract and claim back the deposit because there was a misrepresentation about the age of the watch.
